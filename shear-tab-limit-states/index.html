Shear tab limit states

AISC Manual Part 10: the limit states of a conventional single-plate shear connection, a ⅜ in tab welded to the column flange and bolted to the beam web with four ¾ in bolts. Each figure builds the connection, bolts it and loads the beam, then lets the mechanism develop. Displacements are exaggerated.

35

Bolt shear

The eccentric reaction shears the bolt group; the beam drops and turns about the group’s instantaneous centre, and the bolts shear where the tab meets the web, drawn hidden.

    36

    Tab bearing and tear-out

    Heads left off · The web carries the bolts down; the tab’s holes stretch below them, and the tab tears out to the next hole and from the lowest hole to its bottom edge.

      37

      Web bearing and tear-out

      Tab behind the web, nuts left off · The beam drops past the bolts; the web’s holes stretch above them and the web tears out up to the hole above.

        38

        Tab shear yielding

        The tab yields in shear on its gross section between the weld and the bolts, and the bolt line drops with the beam.

          39

          Tab shear rupture

          The tab tears along the line of bolts, through the holes, and the strip outside it drops with the beam.

            40

            Tab block shear

            A block of the tab outside the bolts and below the top bolt tears out: shear down the bolt line, tension across to the free edge.

              41

              Weld rupture

              The fillet welds tear, and the tab slides down the column flange with the beam.

                42

                Web block shear

                Coped beam, tab behind the web, nuts left off · The corner of the web inside the bolts stays on them as the beam drops: shear up the bolt line to the cope, tension across to the beam’s end.
