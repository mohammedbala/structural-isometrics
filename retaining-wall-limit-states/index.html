1807

Retaining wall

A cantilever retaining wall, 16 ft high on a 10 ft footing: first its stability as a block, against overturning, sliding, bearing and a deep slip circle (IBC 1807.2.3), then the strength of its concrete, the stem in flexure and shear and the heel and toe in flexure (ACI 318). Each figure sets the wall, places the backfill, applies the earth pressure, then lets the mechanism develop. Displacements are exaggerated.

51

Overturning

The thrust of the backfill tips the wall forward about its toe; the heel lifts off and the active wedge slides down its plane behind the wall.

    52

    Sliding

    The wall slides forward on its base; the active wedge drops in behind it and a passive wedge in front of the toe is shoved up its plane.

      53

      Bearing capacity

      The eccentric load crowds the bearing pressure under the toe until the soil there shears out on a curved surface; the toe sinks, the wall tilts and the ground in front heaves.

        54

        Global stability

        A deep slip circle passes under the whole wall; the wall, the backfill and the ground inside it turn together, the backfill dropping and the ground in front heaving.

          55

          Stem flexure

          The stem cantilevers off the footing. The earth pressure bends it forward, and it cracks on the soil face at its base, where the moment is largest.

            56

            Stem shear

            Near the base the shear opens a diagonal crack from the soil face down toward the front face, and the stem above slides on it.

              57

              Heel flexure

              The soil on the heel outweighs the pressure under it; the heel bends down off the stem and cracks on top at the stem face.

                58

                Toe flexure

                The bearing pressure, highest under the toe, bends the toe up off the stem; it cracks underneath at the stem face.
